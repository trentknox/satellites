Caught in the Act: Probes Effectively Detect Sabotage and Catch Unverbalized Deception

Submitted 2026-10-08 · arXiv cs.AI

White-box probes for detecting deception, trained on the largest deception dataset to date with an architecture that aggregates across layers and tokens. The authors report 98.8% AUC in SHADE-Arena, above a frontier text-monitoring baseline, with efficacy improving as the monitored model scales. Signal: monitoring is moving inside the model rather than beside it.

Ecology of AI Agents: Collaboration Creates a Population Threshold for Takeoff

Submitted 2026-10-08 · arXiv cs.AI

Models whether a population of misaligned agents stays contained or enters a self-reinforcing expansion cycle, and identifies a critical population-size threshold below which populations stay bounded. Signal: multi-agent risk has a measurable tipping point.

On the estimation and validity of AI time horizons — a statistical look at the METR plot

Submitted 2026-10-08 · arXiv cs.AI

Recomputes METR's 50% time-horizon measure across 228 tasks and 26 models, relaxing the assumption that task difficulty depends linearly on log human time. The fitted curve is nearly flat between 2 and 30 minutes, which changes how a headline capability jump should be read. Signal: the most-quoted capability chart is sensitive to its modelling assumption.

→ arxiv.org

Submitted 2026-10-08 · arXiv cs.AI

Asks how fine-tuning a model on one narrow set of values changes its behaviour on held-out values, and whether that generalisation can be predicted in advance from the model's value representations. Signal: alignment work is shifting from scoring behaviour to predicting it.
